## Why the signs have shifted

Seen from Earth, the Sun moves through the year along a path against the background stars called the ecliptic. This path is the projection on the sky of Earth's orbit around the Sun, so it returns to the same point once a year. The constellations that straddle this path form the zodiac band, which is also where the Moon and the planets are found. The Sun crosses the celestial equator twice a year, at the March and September equinoxes, and the astrological (tropical) zodiac is anchored to these points: the sign of Aries begins at the March equinox, around 21 March.

The equinoxes are not fixed against the stars. Earth's rotation axis slowly changes direction, much like the axis of a spinning top, in a cycle of about 26,000 years called precession. As a result the equinox moves backwards along the ecliptic, by roughly 30 degrees, the width of one sign, in about 2,150 years. The tropical signs have stayed tied to the seasons, while the constellations they were named after have drifted away from them. The result is a mismatch of about a month: when astrology says the Sun is in Aries, it is actually in front of the stars of Pisces, as the table below shows.

## Thirteen constellations, not twelve

The ecliptic crosses thirteen constellations, including Ophiuchus, which astrology leaves out. The International Astronomical Union fixed the official constellation boundaries in 1930, and these boundaries define how long the Sun spends in each one. The constellations are very unequal in width: the Sun takes 44 days to cross Virgo but only 7 to cross Scorpius. The astrological scheme, by contrast, divides the year into twelve nearly equal parts, which is why the two systems cannot line up. Ophiuchus, the serpent bearer, is crossed by the Sun for 18 days each December, between Scorpius and Sagittarius.

The table compares the two systems. The astronomical dates follow the IAU boundaries.

| Sign | Astrological (tropical) dates | Astronomical (IAU boundaries) dates | Days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aries | 21 Mar–20 Apr | 19 Apr–14 May | 25 |
| Taurus | 21 Apr–21 May | 15 May–21 Jun | 38 |
| Gemini | 22 May–21 Jun | 22 Jun–20 Jul | 30 |
| Cancer | 22 Jun–22 Jul | 21 Jul–10 Aug | 21 |
| Leo | 23 Jul–23 Aug | 11 Aug–16 Sep | 37 |
| Virgo | 24 Aug–22 Sep | 17 Sep–31 Oct | 44 |
| Libra | 23 Sep–23 Oct | 1 Nov–23 Nov | 23 |
| Scorpius | 24 Oct–22 Nov | 24 Nov–30 Nov | 7 |
| Ophiuchus | — | 1 Dec–18 Dec | 18 |
| Sagittarius | 23 Nov–22 Dec | 19 Dec–19 Jan | 31 |
| Capricornus | 23 Dec–20 Jan | 20 Jan–16 Feb | 28 |
| Aquarius | 21 Jan–19 Feb | 17 Feb–11 Mar | 24 |
| Pisces | 20 Feb–20 Mar | 12 Mar–18 Apr | 38 |

## A note on the dates

Dates in both columns can vary by up to two days from year to year, following the leap-year cycle. Treat them as approximate.

## See it yourself

Free planetarium software such as [Stellarium](https://stellarium.org/) shows the Sun's position against the constellations for any date and place, which makes it easy to check the table.
